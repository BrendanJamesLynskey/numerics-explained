<Layer kind="concept">

Every dot product, every matrix multiply and every normalisation is a long sum, and every addition in a sum is rounded. Most of the time the errors are tiny and cancel. In low precision they can do something much worse: **stop the sum altogether**.

The animation adds <V of="demo.sumN" fmt="int" /> numbers between 0 and 1. The exact total is <V of="sum.fp16.uniform.exact" fmt="num" />. Added one at a time in FP16 the running total climbs normally until it reaches 2,048, where FP16's ulp becomes 2: from then on every input is less than half an ulp, every addition rounds back down, and the sum stays at <V of="sum.fp16.uniform.naive" fmt="int" /> for good, half the true answer. In BF16, with three fewer mantissa bits, the same thing happens already at <V of="sum.bf16.uniform.naive" fmt="int" />. This is the stagnation of [chapter 2](/learn/02-rounding), with a growing sum in place of $h$.

Three fixes, all running in the same animation:

- **Accumulate in a wider format.** The FP32 running total ends at <V of="sum.fp16.uniform.naive32" fmt="num" />. This is what hardware does: tensor cores multiply FP16 or BF16 inputs and can add the products into an FP32 accumulator, the usual choice.
- **Kahan summation** keeps a second number, the compensation $c$, that catches the low-order bits each addition throws away and feeds them back into the next one. Even with every operation in FP16 it reaches <V of="sum.fp16.uniform.kahan" fmt="int" />, the representable FP16 value nearest the exact total.
- **Pairwise summation** adds the numbers in a balanced tree: pairs, then pairs of pairs. Partial sums stay comparable in size to the numbers being added, so no input is swamped. It reaches <V of="sum.fp16.uniform.pairwise" fmt="int" />, and this ordering is what numpy's `sum` uses (in blocks, along the fast axis) and what parallel reductions on a GPU do naturally.

Try "all ones": the naive FP16 sum stops at exactly <V of="sum.fp16.ones.naive" fmt="int" />, because $2048 + 1$ is a tie and ties go to the even neighbour, 2,048. Try "± normal": the sum wanders around zero, the errors are smaller, and the order of the additions still changes the answer.

</Layer>

<Layer kind="maths">

With unit roundoff $u = 2^{-(m+1)}$ (half an ulp at 1), the computed naive sum $\hat s$ of $x_1, \dots, x_n$ satisfies (Higham, 1993)

$$
|\hat s - s| \;\le\; (n-1)\,u \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i| + O(u^2),
$$

an error that grows with $n$. Pairwise summation replaces $n - 1$ by $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil$, the depth of the tree, and Kahan's compensated sum removes the dependence on $n$ to first order:

$$
|\hat s_{\text{Kahan}} - s| \;\le\; \bigl(2u + O(n u^2)\bigr) \sum_{i=1}^{n} |x_i| .
$$

The stagnation point follows from the spacing: once the running sum $S$ has $\operatorname{ulp}(S) > 2 \max_i |x_i|$, every input is below half an ulp and $\mathrm{round}(S + x_i) = S$. For FP16 and inputs below 1 that happens at $S = 2^{11} = 2048$, where the ulp becomes 2.

</Layer>

<Layer kind="code">

Kahan's loop with every operation rounded to the format, cut from [`src/lib/num/model.ts`](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/numerics-explained/blob/main/src/lib/num/model.ts):

```ts
for (const x of xs) {
  const y = roundTo(roundTo(x, fid) - c, fid);
  const t = addIn(s, y, fid);
  c = roundTo(roundTo(t - s, fid) - y, fid);
  s = t;
}
```

And the pairwise tree (the left part is the largest power of two below $n$, so the tree over 8,192 inputs is perfectly balanced):

```ts
if (n === 1) return roundTo(xs[lo]!, fid);
let p = 1;
while (p * 2 < n) p *= 2;
return addIn(
  sumPairwise(xs, fid, lo, lo + p),
  sumPairwise(xs, fid, lo + p, hi),
  fid,
);
```

`addIn(a, b)` rounds the exact double sum $a + b$ to the format once, which is exactly the format's own addition (the tests check it against numpy's `float16` and ml_dtypes' `bfloat16` arithmetic).

</Layer>

The inputs are FP16 numbers from a seeded generator, so their exact running sums are exact in double precision. The FP16 and BF16 totals round after every single addition, as a scalar loop would; a GPU kernel accumulates in FP32 and in an order of its own, which is the point of the first fix.
